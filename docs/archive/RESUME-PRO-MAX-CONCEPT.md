Resume Pro Max: concept explainer
A briefing for marketing. What the feature is, why it matters, how to talk about it and what not to claim.

In one line
Your resume, with the proof attached. One PDF: your resume exactly as you wrote it, followed by a verified report of what the people you have worked with actually say about you.

The problem it solves
Every resume makes the same promises. "Results-driven." "Team player." "Strong stakeholder management." A recruiter reads hundreds of them, and every word was written by the person who benefits from it.

The proof that would settle it, what former managers and colleagues think, arrives last. It comes at the very end of a hiring process, over phone calls, if it arrives at all. By then the shortlist has already been made on self-description alone.

So candidates are judged first on the half they write about themselves, and last on the half that others would say.

The idea
Put both halves in the same file, from the very first touch.

Resume Pro Max takes the candidate's own resume and appends their GoodWord report: verified references from people who have worked with them, in those people's own words. The candidate sends one PDF. The recruiter opens one PDF. The claim and the evidence arrive together.

The half you write, and the half you can't.

What is in the file
Part one: the resume. Exactly as the candidate uploaded it. Not reformatted, not rewritten, not "improved". Not a word changed.

Part two: the GoodWord report, rebuilt fresh every time the file is made, so it always reflects the latest references. It contains:

Validated strengths and areas for improvement, drawn from what the referees wrote, never inflated beyond it.
A summary written from the referees' own words.
"Would they want this person on their team again?" Every referee is asked this privately. The report shows the tally, for example "All 3 referees would want Priya on their team again."
Each referee's written response, shown by their role (for example "Direct Manager") unless they chose to be named.
When each reference was given, to the month, so a recruiter knows how current it is.
Response Integrity: a single word (Strong, Good, Moderate or Basic) describing how confident we are that these are real, independent references. It is based on verification signals, never on how positive the feedback is.
A report ID and verify link on every page, so anyone holding the file can check it at source.
Who it is for, and when
For job seekers who want to stand out at the moment it counts most: the first time a real person looks at them.

It is built for sending to a human:

replying to a recruiter who reached out
emailing a hiring manager directly
a referral from a friend inside the company
a WhatsApp or LinkedIn message to someone who asked "send me your CV"
walking into an interview with a printed copy
Why it works
Proof travels with the claim. The recruiter does not have to ask for references or chase anyone. The evidence is already on the page they are reading.

It cannot be self-written. The candidate cannot edit what their referees said. That is the whole point, and it is why the second half carries weight the first half cannot.

It is checkable. Every report page carries a verify link. A recruiter can confirm the report is authentic without going back to the candidate.

Honesty is built in. Every reference includes a growth area, by design. A report that only praised would read like marketing. The candid part is what makes the rest believable.

It is human. It brings the people behind a career back into the application: the manager who watched them grow, the peer who shipped alongside them.

The privacy promise
This is a genuine selling point and it is true in the product:

We never store the resume. It is read, merged with the report and handed straight back in a single step, then it is gone. No copy on our servers, nothing in our database, nothing to delete later. We do not read it, score it or index it. It is carried through as pages.

Guardrails: what not to claim
These matter. Overclaiming would undermine the one thing this product sells, which is trust.

Do not say referees are "identity-verified" or "ID-checked". They are confirmed by email through a private, single-use link. Say that.
Do not position it for job portals or ATS uploads. Automated systems read the whole document, and extra pages can dilute keyword matching or get cut off. The product itself tells candidates to use their plain resume on portals and send Resume Pro Max to people. Marketing should say the same: for humans, not for bots.
Do not imply we improve, rewrite, format or optimise the resume. We do not touch it, and that is part of the promise.
Do not describe it as a rating, score or ranking. GoodWord deliberately has no scores.
Do not say the PDF cannot be altered. Like any PDF, it can be edited once someone has it. The verify link is the authoritative version, and that is the honest answer if anyone asks.
Do not promise it is available to everyone today. It unlocks once a candidate has references and has unlocked their report.
Fine print
Resume must be a PDF, up to 4 MB and up to 15 pages, and not password-protected.
Word documents are not accepted directly; export to PDF first.
The report half needs a live GoodWord report. Individual written responses appear once a candidate has at least 3 references; below that, recruiters see the summary, strengths and verification facts.
A GoodWord report is valid for 12 months.
Naming
Resume Pro Max is the product name, shown in the app with a small gold mark to signal it is the premium part of GoodWord. Because the name does not explain itself, lead with what it does ("your resume with the proof attached") and let the name be the label.

The downloaded file is named plainly, for example priya-sharma-resume-with-references.pdf, so a recruiter knows what they are opening before they open it.

The feeling to leave people with
Job applications have become a contest of adjectives. Resume Pro Max puts real people back into the decision. The candidate is no longer asking to be believed. They are showing what others already believe about them.
