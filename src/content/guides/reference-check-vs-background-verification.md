## Side by side

| | Background verification | Reference check |
|---|---|---|
| The question it answers | Is what you told us true? | What are you like to work with? |
| Who runs it | HR or a verification agency the employer hires | The recruiter or the hiring manager |
| Who they contact | HR at your past employers, universities and official records | The managers, peers and clients you name |
| When | Usually after the offer, before you join or soon after | After you are shortlisted or close to an offer |
| What you give | Your written consent, ID and documents | Names and contact details, with each person's permission |
| What comes back | Match or mismatch against your records | One person's view, with examples |

You will often hear background verification called BGV.

## What a background verification covers

It varies by employer and by role. These are the common checks.

- **Identity.** A government ID such as Aadhaar, PAN or a passport.
- **Employment history.** For each past employer: that you worked there, your designation and your joining and leaving dates. Some also ask your reason for leaving and whether the company would hire you again.
- **Education.** Your degree, institution and year, checked with the university or board.
- **Address.** Your current address and sometimes your permanent one.
- **Court and police records.** More common in finance, security and healthcare roles.

Many employers also look at your EPFO record through your UAN. It lists every employer that paid into your provident fund and the months they paid. That makes overlapping jobs and wrong dates easy to spot.

An employer needs your written consent before running these checks. You will usually sign a form with your offer. A verification commonly takes between one and four weeks.

## What a reference check asks

It is a conversation, usually a phone call of ten or fifteen minutes. Expect questions like these.

- How do you know this person and for how long did you work together?
- What do they do better than most?
- Where could they improve?
- How do they handle pressure or a disagreement?
- Would you work with them again?

Some employers hand this to an agency too. Many do it themselves, because the hiring manager wants to hear the answers.

## Can one replace the other?

No. A clean background verification says your resume is accurate. It says nothing about how you treat a team. A glowing reference says people liked working with you. It does not confirm your dates or your degree.

## How to get ready for a background verification

1. **Make your dates agree.** Your resume, your LinkedIn profile and your letters should show the same joining and leaving dates, to the month.
2. **Check your EPFO service history.** Log in to the EPFO member portal with your UAN and look at the exit date for each employer. A missing exit date is a common cause of delay. Your previous employer can update it.
3. **Collect your documents.** Offer and relieving letters, payslips and degree certificates.
4. **Declare gaps and overlaps yourself.** A gap you explain is rarely a problem. A gap someone else finds looks like something you hid.
5. **Tell HR early about anything missing.** If an old employer never gave you a letter, say so before the check begins. [This guide](/guides/reference-letter-vs-experience-letter-vs-relieving-letter/) lists what else you can offer.

## How to get ready for a reference check

Line up three people before anyone asks. Tell each one the role, the company and when a call is likely. Our guide on [how to ask for a reference](/guides/how-to-ask-for-a-reference/) has the messages. If your manager has moved on, [read this one](/guides/reference-when-your-manager-has-left/).

## If something does not match

Most mismatches are small: a date that is a month out or a designation that changed during your time there. Write to HR, explain it in two lines and attach the document that shows the right detail.

A message you can adapt:

> Hi, I wanted to flag one thing before my verification completes. My resume shows my last working day at Lumen Pay as 30 June 2024. My relieving letter says 12 July 2024, because I served two extra weeks of notice. I have attached the letter. Please tell me if you need anything else.

This is general guidance and not legal advice.
