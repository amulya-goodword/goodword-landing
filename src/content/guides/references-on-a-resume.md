## Why they do not belong on a resume

**The space is better spent.** A resume gets a short first read. Every line should be evidence of what you have done. "References available on request" says nothing a recruiter did not already assume.

**It exposes other people.** A resume uploaded to a job portal is seen by many recruiters and often by people you never applied to. Your former manager did not agree to have their phone number passed around like that.

**You lose control of the timing.** If the names are on the page, anyone can call at any point. Your referee may get a call about a job you have not told them about or one you have already turned down.

**Recruiters ask later anyway.** In most hiring in India, references come up after you are shortlisted or close to an offer. That is when to share them.

## The exceptions

Follow the instructions when an employer gives them.

- **The posting or the form asks for referees.** Then include them, in the format requested.
- **Academic and research roles.** These often expect referees on the CV itself.
- **Some government and overseas applications.** Many have their own reference format. Use it exactly.

## What to do instead

### 1. Line up three people now

Not when the recruiter asks. A manager, a peer and one more person who saw your work closely is a good set. Our guide on [how to ask for a reference](/guides/how-to-ask-for-a-reference/) has messages you can copy.

### 2. Keep a reference sheet ready

One page, separate from your resume, sent only when someone asks for it.

> References for Aarav Mehta
>
> Neha Kapoor
> Director of Product, Lumen Pay
> My manager from 2022 to 2025
> neha.kapoor@example.com · 98XXXXXX10
>
> Rohan Iyer
> Engineering Lead, Lumen Pay
> We worked on the same product for two years
> rohan.iyer@example.com · 98XXXXXX22

Add contact details only with each person's permission.

### 3. Send it with a note

> Hi Ritika, as requested, here are three people who can speak about my work. All of them know you may be in touch. Neha was my manager most recently and is the best person to speak to first. She is easiest to reach after 4 pm.

### 4. Warn your referees

A message the day you share their details is enough. Tell them the company, the role and who might call.

### 5. Put their words where recruiters already look

A recommendation on your LinkedIn profile is public and stays with you. It is worth asking for one at the end of a good project.

## If you are a fresher

Most campus hiring does not ask for references at all. If a form does, a professor, a project guide or an internship supervisor is the right person. Ask them the same way you would ask a manager.
