## When to follow up

- **After applying:** about a week later. Sooner looks impatient. Much later and the role may be closed.
- **After an interview:** a thank-you note the same day, then a follow-up on the date they gave you for a decision. If they gave none, wait about a week.
- **After a second message with no reply:** stop. Silence is an answer, even if it is a rude one.

## Who to write to

A person reads a message. A careers inbox often does not.

- **The recruiter named in the job post,** if there is one.
- **The hiring manager.** On LinkedIn, search the company with the team name and a title like "head of" or "manager".
- **Someone you know at the company,** who can pass your name along. Our guide on [the best way to get a job](/guides/best-way-to-get-a-job/) covers asking for a referral.

## What to say

Short, specific and easy to answer. Always name the role.

### Email, a week after applying

> Subject: Application for Senior Analyst, job ID 4471
>
> Hi Ritika,
>
> I applied for the Senior Analyst role on 10 October and wanted to check whether it is still open. I have four years in retail analytics and led the pricing dashboard used by 40 stores, which seems close to what the role needs.
>
> My resume is attached again for convenience. I would welcome a short call if the profile fits.
>
> Regards,
> Aarav

### LinkedIn message to a recruiter

> Hi Ritika, I applied for the Senior Analyst role last week. I have four years in retail analytics, including the pricing work that the role mentions. Happy to share more if it helps. Thank you for your time.

### After an interview

> Hi Arjun, thank you for the conversation today. I enjoyed hearing about the plans for the new warehouse system. It made me even keener on the role. Please let me know if you need anything else from me.

### The second and last follow-up

> Hi Ritika, following up once more on the Senior Analyst role. If it has been filled, no problem at all. I would be glad to be considered for similar roles in future.

## What to attach

Something new, not just the same resume. A short portfolio, a one-page case study or references from people you have worked with all give the recruiter a reason to look again.

## What not to do

- Message every day.
- Call repeatedly, unless they asked you to call.
- Send the same copied message to ten people at the same company.
- Sound annoyed at the silence. It never helps.
- Use WhatsApp unless the recruiter contacted you there first.

A follow-up works best when it adds something. A GoodWord report is one option: what three people who worked with you say about your work, in one link. A recruiter confirms a work email to open it. You can see who viewed it. See [a sample report](https://app.goodword.tech/sample).
